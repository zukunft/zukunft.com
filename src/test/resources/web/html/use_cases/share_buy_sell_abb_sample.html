ABB shares (SIX: ABBN): buy, sell or hold at 72.88 CHF?

... more

ABB Ltd of Zurich sells electrification and automation equipment, reports in US dollars and is quoted in Swiss francs; 1.81 billion shares are outstanding, the market value is about 130 billion CHF [1][9]. The Q3 2026 results of 20 October 2026 brought record orders of 12.1 billion USD and a record operational EBITA margin of 20.8 %, but the adjusted earnings per share missed the consensus by 13 % and the share fell from a 52-week high of 89.14 CHF to 72.88 CHF at the close of 28 October [1][2][9]. Nine analysts rate the share hold with targets from 57 to 88 CHF, on average 77 CHF [3]. Behind the figures lie three portfolio moves: the sale of Robotics to SoftBank for 5.375 billion USD closing in Q4 2026, the 5.5 billion USD offer for the British valve maker Rotork closing in the first half of 2027, and a 51 % stake in Mitsubishi Electric's low-voltage business [1][5][11][13]. Below are the arguments of the bulls and the bears with their numbers, then a calculator: it derives a target price from five valuation methods (earnings multiple, discounted cash flow, enterprise value multiple, dividend discount, analyst consensus), weights them as you say, compares the result with the price - and tells you whether that suggests buying, holding or selling under your assumptions. A model is not investment advice; the assumptions are the decision.

Arguments for buying (bull case)

  • Record order book Q3 orders 12.1 billion USD, up 40 %, book-to-bill 1.24, backlog 32.3 billion USD, up 35 %; data-centre orders grew by a triple-digit percentage. The revenue of 2027 is largely in the books before it is sold. [1]

  • Record profitability Operational EBITA margin 20.8 % in Q3 and 21.4 % over nine months, guidance for 2026 of at least 270 basis points above 2025; return on capital employed 29.9 %, return on equity 33 %. [1][2]
  • Cash machine with an unlevered balance sheet Free cash flow 3.7 billion USD over nine months and about 5 billion USD for the year; net debt only 1.3 billion USD, 0.2 times EBITA; a 2 billion USD buyback runs until January 2027 and the CFO sees 13 to 14 billion USD of debt capacity. [1][2][4]
  • Portfolio shifted to the higher-margin businesses Robotics (12 % margin) sold for 5.375 billion USD with a 2.4 billion USD gain in Q4; Rotork (flow control) bought for 5.5 billion USD; Mitsubishi Electric low-voltage JV at 11 times EBITDA with a 20 % margin target. [1][5][11][13]
  • The price has already corrected 18 % below the 52-week high of 89.14 CHF and more than 10 % down over the past month; a discounted cash flow model on Simply Wall St sees the share 14 % undervalued, Yahoo's one-year target estimate is 83.5 CHF. [6][10]
  • Rising dividend 0.94 CHF for 2025 after 0.90 CHF, raised every year; 1.3 % yield plus the buyback, and the proceeds of Robotics still to be deployed. [4]

Arguments for selling (bear case)

  • Priced for perfection Trailing P/E about 32, 23 on the 2027 estimate, EV/EBITA about 19; nine analysts, nine hold ratings, average target 77 CHF only 6 % above the price, Jefferies at 57 CHF. [3][9][10]

  • Earnings miss Adjusted EPS 0.69 USD against 0.79 USD expected, 13 % short; reported EPS 0.65 USD, down 2 %; revenue slightly below consensus; the share lost 5 % on the day. [2]
  • Orders do not fully reach the bottom line Bernstein: revenue and margin "did not quite hold" what the orders promised; gross margin down 0.6 points to 40.6 %, Motion margin down to 19.1 %, Gamesa Electric loss-making. [6][7]
  • Acquisition risk Rotork at a full price with closing only in H1 2027, a deal-contingent hedge already cost 115 million USD in Q3; the Japanese JV adds integration work. [1][2]
  • Cycle and AI dependency Record margins tend to revert; Q4 margin guided to soften; the triple-digit data-centre growth is the first to fall if AI capital spending slows. [1]
  • Currency drag Earnings in USD, share in CHF: the dollar is down to about 0.83 CHF; every cent lost cuts the CHF earnings and dividend capacity. [8]

Formulas and sources

currency formula ABB reports in US dollars and the share is quoted in Swiss francs, so every per-share figure in USD is multiplied by the USD/CHF rate before it meets the price

earnings multiple formula the target by the earnings multiple: the earnings per share 2027 times the fair price-earnings ratio, in CHF

free cash flow per share formula the free cash flow of 2026 divided by the shares outstanding; billion USD divided by billion shares gives USD per share

discounted cash flow formula two stages: the free cash flow per share grows at the explicit rate for the number of years set and each year's cash flow is discounted at the required return; after the last year a terminal value is the next year's cash flow divided by the required return minus the terminal growth, discounted over the explicit years; the net debt per share is subtracted because the cash flow belongs to the creditors first; the only loop of this page

enterprise value formula the operational EBITA 2026 times the fair EV/EBITA multiple gives the enterprise value; minus the net debt gives the equity value; divided by the shares and converted gives the target per share

dividend discount formula the Gordon growth model: the dividend per share times one plus the growth, divided by the required return minus the growth; undefined if the growth is not below the return

weighted target formula each method's target times its weight, summed and divided by the sum of the weights of the methods that produced a value; a method without a value (dividend discount with growth above the return) is left out

upside formula the target divided by the price minus one, in percent; positive means the share is below its target

share expected return formula the annual return if the price reaches the target over the holding period: the ratio of target to price to the power of one over the years, minus one, plus the dividend yield

flip price formula buy below the target divided by one plus the margin of safety; sell above the target divided by one minus the margin of safety

key figure formulas trailing P/E: price divided by the last twelve months' EPS in CHF; forward P/E: price divided by the EPS 2027 in CHF; PEG: forward P/E divided by the earnings growth from 2026 to 2027 in percent; EV/EBITA: market capitalisation in USD plus net debt, divided by the EBITA 2026; free cash flow yield: free cash flow per share in CHF divided by the price; dividend yield: dividend divided by the price

verdict formula buy if the upside is above the margin of safety, sell if it is below minus the margin of safety, hold in between; the margin is an assumption of the reader

model limits one price on one day against estimates that move every quarter; the EPS 2026 consensus may or may not contain the 2.4 billion USD Robotics gain and is used for the PEG only; the Rotork outflow of 2027 and the Robotics inflow of Q4 2026 are not in the net debt; no tax on the dividend, no transaction cost, no portfolio context, no scenario probabilities; the analysts' models, the ABB financial report and the quarterly press releases are the places to look for these
