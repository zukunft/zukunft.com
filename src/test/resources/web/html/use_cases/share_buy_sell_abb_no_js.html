ABB shares (SIX: ABBN): buy, sell or hold at 72.88 CHF?

ABB Ltd of Zurich sells electrification and automation equipment, reports in US dollars and is quoted in Swiss francs; 1.81 billion shares are outstanding, the market value is about 130 billion CHF [1][9]. The Q3 2026 results of 20 October 2026 brought record orders of 12.1 billion USD and a record operational EBITA margin of 20.8 %, but the adjusted earnings per share missed the consensus by 13 % and the share fell from a 52-week high of 89.14 CHF to 72.88 CHF at the close of 28 October [1][2][9]. Nine analysts rate the share hold with targets from 57 to 88 CHF, on average 77 CHF [3]. Behind the figures lie three portfolio moves: the sale of Robotics to SoftBank for 5.375 billion USD closing in Q4 2026, the 5.5 billion USD offer for the British valve maker Rotork closing in the first half of 2027, and a 51 % stake in Mitsubishi Electric's low-voltage business [1][5][11][13]. Each cell: the weighted target price in CHF and, in brackets, the upside against the current price in percent with all other assumptions as set on the left (green: buy, red: sell, grey: hold within the margin of safety); only the earnings multiple and the PEG change along the grid, the other methods stay as set

EPS growth formula the earnings growth from the 2026 estimate to the 2027 estimate, as a share

PEG formula the forward P/E divided by the earnings growth in percent; the growth is stored as a share, so the factor 100 turns it into the percentage points the PEG convention uses

buy threshold formula buy below the target divided by one plus the margin of safety

discounted cash flow target two stages: the free cash flow per share grows at the explicit rate for the number of years set and each year's cash flow is discounted at the required return; after the last year a terminal value is the next year's cash flow divided by the required return minus the terminal growth, discounted over the explicit years; the net debt per share is subtracted because the cash flow belongs to the creditors first. The sums of the discount factors of the two stages need a power, which the zukunft.com formulas do not have, so they are stored as the modelled present value factors: the explicit stage factor is the sum over the years t = 1 to n of ((1 + growth) / (1 + return)) to the power of t, the terminal factor is (1 + growth) to the power of n times (1 + terminal growth) divided by (return - terminal growth) divided by (1 + return) to the power of n

dividend discount target the Gordon growth model: the dividend per share times one plus the growth, divided by the required return minus the growth; undefined if the growth is not below the return

dividend yield formula the dividend per share divided by the price

earnings multiple target the target by the earnings multiple: the earnings per share 2027 times the fair price-earnings ratio, in CHF

enterprise value multiple formula the market capitalisation in USD plus the net debt, divided by the operational EBITA 2026

enterprise value multiple target the operational EBITA 2026 times the fair EV/EBITA multiple gives the enterprise value; minus the net debt gives the equity value; divided by the shares and converted gives the target per share

forward P/E formula the price divided by the EPS 2027 in CHF

free cash flow per share formula the free cash flow of 2026 divided by the shares outstanding; billion USD divided by billion shares gives USD per share, times the exchange rate CHF per share

free cash flow yield formula the free cash flow per share in CHF divided by the price

market capitalisation formula the price times the shares outstanding; CHF times billion shares gives billion CHF

method upside formula the upside of one method's target against the price, the vs. price column of the method rows

net debt per share formula the net debt divided by the shares outstanding, in CHF

sell threshold formula sell above the target divided by one minus the margin of safety

share expected return formula the annual return if the price reaches the target over the holding period: the annualised price return, a modelled value because it needs the power of one over the years, plus the dividend yield

trailing P/E formula the price divided by the last twelve months' EPS in CHF

upside formula the target divided by the price minus one, as a share; positive means the share is below its target; the percent measure shows it as a percentage

weighted target price formula each method's target times its weight, summed and divided by the sum of the weights of the methods that produced a value; a method without a value (dividend discount with growth above the return) is left out by the page
