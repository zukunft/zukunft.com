New nuclear power plants in Switzerland: the vote of 28 February 2027

... more

The Swiss electorate decides whether the ban on building new nuclear power plants (NPP), in force since 2018, is lifted again. Parliament passed the amendment of the Nuclear Energy Act as the indirect counter-proposal to the "Blackout stoppen" initiative; the referendum against it was filed with about 132'000 signatures in October 2026 . A yes only allows new plants; the build decision would follow in the 2030s and a plant would run from about 2050 [1] [2] . Below are the arguments of both sides with their numbers, then a calculator: set your own assumptions with the sliders and see whether a new plant, under these assumptions, lowers or raises the average electricity cost in 2050 - and what that suggests for your vote.

Arguments for lifting the ban (yes)

  • Winter supply gap The four existing plants deliver 36 % of Swiss electricity and almost half of the winter electricity; the last one (Leibstadt) is expected to stop around 2044, while demand rises to about 76 TWh by 2050. Without new plants the winter net import grows to 7.5 TWh in 2050; one EPR cuts it to 3.2 TWh . [2]
... more
  • Competitive if the construction cost stays moderate With state support and financing at 5 % instead of 8 %, two of four ETH/PSI models build 2 GW at 8'000 CHF/kW , and all four build 2.6 to 4.9 GW at 5'000 CHF/kW. [1]
  • Lower and steadier wholesale prices A new plant lowers the average wholesale price by 7 to 9 CHF/MWh and dampens winter peaks of 130 to 135 CHF/MWh ; the avoided price volatility, outage and shortage risks are valued at about 520 million CHF per year. [2]
  • Generation cost in the range of renewables Levelised cost 2050 of a new plant 80 to 155 CHF/MWh versus 60 to 130 CHF/MWh for rooftop PV and 60 to 100 CHF/MWh for Swiss onshore wind - before the storage, backup and grid cost that intermittent sources add. [2]
  • Climate and land 10 to 20 g CO₂-eq/kWh over the life cycle against about 198 g for today's winter import mix; about 7'500 GWh per km² and year, 54 times the density of rooftop PV. [2]
  • Value added and jobs About 1.6 billion CHF value added and 2'900 full-time jobs per year over 60 years of operation (study commissioned by economiesuisse). [2]
  • An option, not an obligation Lifting the ban keeps the nuclear option open; whether a plant is built is decided later on cost and demand. [1] [3]

Arguments for keeping the ban (no)

  • Not competitive under present conditions 19 energy experts of ETH Zurich and PSI, four models: new plants are too expensive without subsidies and state-borne financing risk, even at 5'000 to 8'000 CHF/kW . At 12'000 CHF/kW , the level of recent projects in Europe and the US, three of four models build none even with support. [1]
... more
  • 14 to 43 billion CHF The Swiss Academies of Sciences put the cost of one plant at 14 billion CHF (Westinghouse AP1000) or 21 billion CHF (EDF EPR) in the optimistic case, 25 or 43 billion CHF if the overruns of Olkiluoto, Flamanville, Hinkley Point and Vogtle repeat. [3]
  • Permanent subsidies Even the pro study calculates a levelised cost of 115.5 CHF/MWh against a market price of 64 CHF/MWh : the market pays 55 %, the state about 625 million CHF per year. No Western new build of the last twenty years was financed without state support. [2]
  • Too late for the gap it is meant to close About 23 years from the vote to commercial operation (2050); the existing plants stop in the 2030s and 2040s. The head of the largest nuclear operator fears ten years of debate with nothing built. [2]
  • Net zero is reachable without All four models reach net zero 2050 with 36 to 43 TWh of solar and 0.3 to 8.6 TWh of wind; hydro and PV form three quarters of the supply. Winter imports stay in every scenario, with or without new plants. [1]
  • No cheaper electricity bill The models do not show a lower energy part of the tariff with new plants than with renewables; subsidies paid through the grid surcharge raise the bill.
  • Accident, waste and cluster risk A residual risk and a single large unit (1.6 GW, about 16 % of demand) whose outage must be covered - the pro study leaves the risk side out; an insurance study prices the risk below 0.0001 Rp/kWh, the opponents call it uninsurable. [2]

Formulas and sources

alternative cost formula the levelised cost of the renewable alternative plus the system cost of intermittent supply per MWh

annual cost with new NPP formula the annual cost of the compared kilowatt-hours with the new plant

annual cost without new NPP formula the annual cost of the same kilowatt-hours from the renewable alternative plus the value of the shifted trade and the secured supply

annual production formula the electricity of the plant in TWh: the capacity in GW times the capacity factor times the hours of a year; GW times hours gives GWh, divided by 1000 TWh

annuity factor formula the share of the investment to recover every year

average cost change formula the size of the average cost difference for the text of the verdict

average cost formula the cost difference spread over all consumption in Rp per kWh: million CHF per TWh is 0.1 Rp per kWh

break-even construction cost formula the overnight construction cost at which both options cost the same: the levelised cost that makes the cost difference zero minus the operating cost, divided by the capital cost per MWh of one CHF per kW

capital cost formula the annual recovery of the investment per MWh: million CHF per TWh equals CHF per MWh

cost difference formula the annual cost with the new NPP minus the annual cost without it in million CHF; negative means the new NPP is cheaper

energy cost with new NPP formula the energy tariff with the new plant is the tariff without it plus the average cost difference

household bill change formula the size of the difference of the household bill for the text of the verdict

household bill formula the annual bill of an H4 household in CHF

household bill without new NPP formula the price times 4'500 kWh without the new plant

household price formula the household price in the scenario with the new NPP: the energy tariff without it plus the average cost difference plus grid, metering and levies; the levies value carries the grid part too

household price without new NPP formula the energy tariff plus grid, metering and levies without the new plant

interest during construction formula the interest on the capital spent before operation in million CHF: CHF per kW times GW is million CHF; the factor is the share of the construction time over which the capital is on average outstanding

investment formula the total capital until commercial operation in million CHF

investment in billion formula the investment of the result table in billion CHF

levelised cost formula capital cost plus operating cost per MWh

security value formula the value of the secured winter supply in million CHF per year: the shifted energy times the value per MWh the reader sets

shifted energy formula the energy the new NPP moves from winter imports to summer exports: its production times the difference of its winter share and the winter share of the renewable alternative

subsidy need formula what the market does not pay, in million CHF per year; zero if the levelised cost is below the market price

trade value formula the value of the shifted trade in million CHF per year: avoided winter imports minus lost summer exports

verdict formula a ratio below -1 is yes (new NPP cheaper by more than the threshold), above +1 is no, in between undecided; the verdict of the page compares the average cost with the threshold

winter net import formula the winter net import with the new NPP: the winter net import without it minus the shifted energy

Model limits annual balance in prices of 2024 for one year of operation around 2050, no hourly dispatch, no learning or escalation of costs over time, no construction delay beyond the construction time set, no risk cost, no CO₂ price, no grid expansion difference between central and decentral supply; the ETH/PSI models and the BAK balance model are the places to look for these effects
