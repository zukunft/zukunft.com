CBAM Part Tree Calculator

Carbon border adjustment for an imported good built from parts, which are built from parts. Each part carries its own production emissions, electricity, transport, capital-goods share and end of life. The result shows the certificates due under the EU rules and, next to it, the full emission chain that the rules leave out.

How the obligation is built

Certificates per tonne of good = embedded emissions − benchmark × (1 − CBAM factor), floored at zero. The benchmark is the EU ETS product benchmark; the free-allocation share (1 − CBAM factor) falls from 97.5 % in 2026 to 0 % in 2034 [2][4].

Embedded emissions under the rules are the direct process emissions of the good and of its CBAM precursors, plus indirect electricity emissions for cement, fertilisers and electricity only [3]. Transport, end of life and capital goods are not in the rules; this page counts them in a second column so the gap is visible.

Default values may be used instead of verified data. They carry a mark-up of 10 % in 2026, 20 % in 2027 and 30 % from 2028 (1 % for fertilisers) [5]. A part declared with a default value counts the whole chain below it as settled.

2 · Bill of parts

Open a part to set its own emissions, electricity, transport, capital goods and end of life, and to add the parts it is made of. Quantities are tonnes of the part per tonne of the part above it. Values are per tonne of the part itself.

What it changes

The certificates to surrender are reduced by the carbon price effectively paid abroad on the same emissions [3]. The deduction is capped at the obligation itself.

Notes

Nesting. A part's emissions per tonne are its own process emissions plus the sum over its parts of quantity × that part's emissions, to any depth. A part declared by default value stops the recursion for the legal column; its parts still contribute transport, capital-goods and end-of-life emissions to the full column.

Benchmark of a complex good. Free allocation is deducted for each step with an EU ETS product benchmark, weighted by quantity; rolling, tube forming or cement grinding have none of their own and are covered by the hot metal or clinker benchmark of their precursors. A good declared by default value gets the benchmark of its whole route. The definitive-period CBAM benchmarks per CN code (Reg. 2025/2620) can differ from the 2021–2025 ETS benchmark values used here as defaults [4].

Capital goods. The emissions of building the plant are spread over the tonnes it produces during its life; this is the depreciation the rules do not require.

End of life. Net = disposal emissions × (1 − recycling rate) − avoided primary production × recycling rate. A negative value is a credit.
